NARRATIVE — the familiar chaos, told plainly, long-form

Every multi-day event runs the same way behind the scenes. A spreadsheet holds the truth — 200-some rows of sessions, speakers, timings, mic counts — and one person holds it in their head. When something slips, that person radios, texts, or jogs across the room to say so. Everyone else is guessing.

The performer backstage doesn't know if they have four minutes or forty. The AV crew doesn't know the next cue needs a lav mic until someone tells them. The presenter can't see the clock from the stage. None of this is anyone's fault — it's what happens when the only source of truth lives in a file nobody else can see.

A spreadsheet can hold the schedule. It can't tell a room what's happening right now.
TRANSITION — the product appears for the first time, modestly sized

So Kramflow puts the schedule on a screen instead — one that updates itself.

SCREENSHOT — operator console next to a small TV display, implied live link between them

One operator runs a live queue. Every connected display — lobby, backstage, AV booth, presenter monitor — reflects it within about a second. No relay. No shouting.

WHY A TIMER APP ISN'T ENOUGH — the real structure underneath

A live event isn't one countdown. It's a structure.

Multiple days. Multiple sessions per day. Sections within a session. And inside each one, a queue item with its own timing, owner, and production needs. Kramflow models that directly — the queue is the core entity; a timer is just one property of one item in it.

For the person running it

An operator — stage manager or AV lead — drives the queue from a laptop or a phone in their pocket. They sign up, create an event, and it's theirs alone. Reorder, bulk-edit, recalculate timing, all while the show is live.

For everyone else in the room

Performers, crew, presenters, attendees — nobody else touches the interface. They scan a QR code, follow a link, or type a 6-digit code on a TV. No account. No app. Just the current state of the show, from fifteen feet away.
