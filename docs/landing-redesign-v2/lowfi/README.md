# Kramflow landing page — 4 low-fidelity concepts

Four structurally different low-fi wireframes (grayscale, system font, plain boxes — structure and content strategy only, no visual design) for the Kramflow marketing landing page. Designed independently from the brief in the task prompt, without looking at the existing site. Open any `concept-*.html` directly in a browser.

## Concept A — "The Queue Itself" (`concept-a.html`)

Leads with the product doing its one job: a large live-state display mockup sits directly under a short headline ("What's live. What's next. On every screen in the room."), everything else compressed into a single above-the-fold-sized hero in the Linear/Huly mold — product as proof, minimal adjectives. A literal fan-out diagram (console → queue → 4 displays) explains the mechanism in one glance before any feature list. Short page, low section count, fast to scan. Optimized for a visitor who already half-understands "live event software" and just needs to see the mechanism and trust it immediately. Main tradeoff: it assumes quick comprehension — it doesn't spend time persuading someone who's skeptical that this is different from a countdown timer, which concepts C and D do more explicitly.

## Concept B — "The Green Room at 7:58pm" (`concept-b.html`)

Leads with the problem, not the product — a narrative hero (a specific, concrete moment of chaos) with no screenshot above the fold at all, then an editorial, long-document pacing: a manifesto-style paragraph block and pull-quote before the product is even shown. The entity model (Event → Session → Section → Queue Item) is explained in prose and a simple nested-box diagram only after the reader has been convinced the problem is real. Proof arrives as a single narrative quote, not a logo wall. Optimized for a cold visitor (found via search or a share) who doesn't yet know they have this problem named — it builds empathy before it asks for anything. Main tradeoff: it's the slowest concept to reach the product and the CTA, which risks losing a visitor who already knows what they want and came to compare features fast.

## Concept C — "Two Audiences, One Queue" (`concept-c.html`)

Leads with a literal split-screen hero — operator console on the left with its own headline and CTA, venue display on the right with its own headline and CTA — addressing both audiences equally in the first fold instead of picking one to lead with. Follows with a nested-structure diagram, a 2×2 grid of the four display surfaces, and an explicit spec-sheet comparison table against generic countdown-timer tools (the inspo research's "feature matrix" pattern), then a dense two-column checklist of operator features. Medium-to-high density throughout, very structural. Optimized for a visitor doing active evaluation — someone comparing Kramflow against StageTimer.io-style tools and needing the differentiation spelled out plainly. Main tradeoff: splitting the hero in half means neither audience gets the full-width, uninterrupted first impression that A or D give the operator.

## Concept D — "Built for the Pressure of a Live Show" (`concept-d.html`)

Leads conventionally (headline, subhead, CTA, modest product screenshot) but immediately backs it with a DigitalOcean-style stat strip (<1s sync, 200+ cue queue, 4 surfaces, 3-day events) directly under the fold, then a logo cloud, two bento-grid feature mosaics (operator features, then the four display surfaces), a dedicated reliability section with a testimonial, and an FAQ accordion before the close. By far the longest and densest of the four, with the most proof points and the most "enterprise B2B" shape — closest to how Vercel or Sourcegraph build trust through repetition and numbers rather than a single strong demonstration. Optimized for a risk-averse buyer (an event producer vetting a tool for a client event, not just a solo stage manager) who wants reassurance stacked before committing. Main tradeoff: length and density cost scroll time and above-the-fold impact — it's the slowest to feel exciting even though it's the most thorough.

## Research notes

Grounded the brief against `PRODUCT.md` and `docs/PRD.md` at the repo root for the real entity model (events → sessions → sections/partitions → queue items), the four named display surfaces (General, AV, Green Room, Presenter), the access model (share link / QR / 6-digit TV code, no login), and the "what's happening now / what's next" framing — did not open any file under `components/site/`, `components/auth/`, `app/page.tsx`, `app/globals.css`, `app/layout.tsx`, `docs/audit-remediation/`, or the running app's `/`, `/login`, `/signup` routes, per the constraint to design independently of the existing landing page.

For reference sites, used the `inspo` MCP tool rather than browsing live sites directly:
- `recommend` on a brief describing a "mission-critical real-time production console, dark-mode, built for people under pressure" surfaced **Feature Stack** as the dominant macrostructure among comparable dark-mode technical SaaS sites (Superlist, Huly, Featurebase, Morgen, Sourcegraph) — informed Concept A's instinct to lead with the product screen itself and keep the hero compressed to one viewport.
- `find_examples_for_macrostructure("stat-led")` returned DigitalOcean, React Summit, and Tinybird — the stat-strip-directly-under-the-hero pattern (big numbers backing a claim, read in under three seconds) directly informed Concept D's reliability framing (<1s sync, 200+ cues, 4 surfaces).
- A web search on Linear/Raycast/Vercel/Attio patterns (via Evil Martians' "100 dev tool landing pages" study and SaaSFrame teardowns) confirmed two divergent proof strategies worth representing as separate concepts: Linear's "show the product, then back it with logos" (→ Concept A) versus Vercel's "minimal hero, proof built up gradually through the page" (→ Concept D's longer, denser structure). The study's note that centered, product-demonstrating heroes read as most trustworthy for dev tools also shaped Concept A directly.
- A search for StageTimer.io-specific design critique turned up little substantive material (mostly the vendor's own changelog), so the comparison-table idea in Concept C is informed by the brief's own positioning language ("the queue is the core entity; timers are a property of a queue item") rather than a direct teardown of StageTimer's site — it is deliberately generic ("countdown-timer tools"), not a StageTimer callout.

No existing Kramflow marketing assets, screenshots, or copy were referenced in building any of the four concepts.
