# KramFlow — Round 2: Real-World / Exploratory QA + UI/UX Report

**Scope:** Same branch/commit as Round 1 (`deep`, `7b1ebe9`), same temp Supabase project (`kramflow-qa-temp`, still alive and healthy from Round 1). Baseline re-confirmed clean (`npm run lint`, `npx tsc --noEmit`) before starting; no source files were changed this round, so `npm run build` was not re-run.

**Method:** In-character persona roleplay via real browser automation (multiple simultaneous tabs, viewport/zoom manipulation, DB-level fault injection to simulate network conditions), not a checklist. Every finding below was reproduced live and then root-caused in source; a few are code-level-only where live reproduction wasn't possible in this environment (called out explicitly).

## Persona narratives

**A — Priya, stage manager, 40 minutes before doors, mildly panicked.**
I opened the operator dashboard on a normal laptop-width window and started treating it the way someone actually would mid-panic: clicking a dialog's confirm button, then immediately clicking somewhere else before the animation settled; hitting Escape on a Jump confirmation instead of reading it; clicking directly on the backdrop instead of Cancel. All of that was handled safely — nothing accidentally committed. Then I did the thing that actually breaks things: I fat-fingered a Jump to item 15 (meant to stay near item 2), confirmed it, and then tried to get back to *exactly* where I was. There's no "undo" — Previous only steps back one from wherever you landed (item 14, not item 2), and the only real recovery is remembering the number and re-typing it into Jump. The good news: item "done" status is computed live from position, not a persisted log, so nothing corrupted — but every display showed the wrong item live for the whole confused window. I also dragged the window narrower out of habit and zoomed to 125% (a very normal "I'm presenting on a projector" reflex) and rediscovered Round 1's header-collapse bug through a completely different, more mundane door than resizing.

**B — Marcus, AV crew, on his phone, hands full of cables.**
Opening `/av` on a phone-width viewport immediately clips the "LIVE" badge 15px off the right edge of the screen — small, but it's the one badge that answers "is this actually live." Rotating to landscape (which people do without thinking) is worse: the display routes are hard-locked to `overflow: hidden` at `h-screen`, so in a short viewport the countdown — the single most important number on the page — gets silently clipped below the fold with no scrollbar and no way to see it. On Green Room the same thing manifests even worse: labels physically overlap each other. Then I simulated Marcus's flaky venue wifi by revoking Supabase read access for ~30 seconds and watched what happened when an operator (a different tab) tried to advance the show during the outage: the click produced no visible error, no toast, nothing — I had to check the database directly to find out the write had actually silently failed. Worse, when I restored access and kept clicking Next from that same operator tab, it kept *looking* like nothing was happening (stuck on the same item, escalating "over" countdown) — but the server had actually advanced multiple times in the background. The operator's own dashboard had silently desynced from reality and needed a hard refresh to catch up, while the public Green Room display (a separate tab) had already recovered on its own and was showing the correct, current item the whole time. The person driving the show was the one with stale information.

**C — a performer glancing at the TV for two seconds.**
The normal "on stage now / next" layout is genuinely good — big, unambiguous, answers the app's own stated question in under two seconds. Two things broke that during testing. First, when the operator engages Hold, the only signal is a small orange "PAUSED" badge in the far top-right corner — the giant countdown number in the center, the thing your eye actually goes to, doesn't change color or style at all, so a 2-second glance could easily miss that the show is paused. Second, and more concretely wrong: once a session is marked finished, both Green Room and AV show "Session Finished" as the headline — directly above a countdown that's frozen at a stale "05:00 remaining" with the caption "REMAINING — COUNTDOWN UNTIL CALLED," as if something were still about to happen. That's a directly contradictory message on the one screen whose entire job is answering "what's happening now."

**D — a first-time volunteer handed a phone and told to use `/remote`.**
The core controls (huge Next button, Hold, the icon+label quick-action row for Jump/Alert/Notes/Broadcast) are genuinely self-explanatory — every icon has a text label, nothing is a mystery-glyph. The one place a newcomer would get stuck: the session tab strip at the top only shows the day name ("Friday," "Saturday" ×3, "Sunday" ×2) with zero session-label text anywhere in the button — visually or in the accessible name. For a multi-day event with three different Saturday sessions, there is no way to tell them apart without tapping one and reading the resulting confirm dialog or header. PIN entry itself is solid — pasting a PIN with stray whitespace works fine, the input strips non-digits before matching.

**E — two operators, unintentionally.**
I opened `/operator` in two tabs (simulating an old, forgotten session left open by a handoff) and fired genuinely conflicting actions seconds apart: Tab A engaged Hold, Tab B (unaware) clicked Next almost immediately after. There is no presence indicator anywhere in the app — confirmed by grep, not just by looking — so neither operator has any way to know someone else is also connected. The conflict resolved silently and unfavorably for Tab A: Next's server-side patch unconditionally clears `paused_at` as a side effect, so Operator B's ordinary "advance the show" action silently cancelled Operator A's Hold with zero indication to either person that it happened. Operator A's own dashboard simply started showing a normal, unpaused countdown a few seconds after they'd clicked Hold, with nothing telling them why.

## Non-bugs / lower-confidence observations (worth knowing, not scored)

- **`live_details_panel`/countdown formatting inconsistency (operator vs. TV displays):** when an item goes into overtime, the Operator Dashboard's own countdown stays plain white text in raw total-minutes format (e.g. "728:08," no hour breakdown, no "+" sign), with only a thin progress bar changing to red and a small gray "over" caption. Both public TV displays (`/av`, `/green-room`) show the same event as bold red text in `+H:MM:SS` format (e.g. "+12:07:41"). The operator — who most needs to notice — gets the weakest, hardest-to-parse version of this signal on their own screen. Not independently scored as a numbered bug since it's a polish/consistency issue rather than a functional break, but it's concrete and reproducible (see Persona A narrative) and worth fixing alongside R2-BUG-1/R2-BUG-2's error-visibility work.
- **Hold state has a weak glance signal:** confirmed live — the only visual change when Hold engages is a small "PAUSED" badge in the header; the large countdown number (the actual focal point) doesn't change color/style. Related to, but distinct from, R2-BUG-6.
- **`prefers-reduced-motion` is not respected anywhere** — confirmed via `grep` on `app/globals.css` (which explicitly documents "no light theme and no `prefers-color-scheme` switch" but has no `forced-colors` or `prefers-reduced-motion` handling either). Not live-tested against an actual OS reduced-motion setting in this environment, but the CSS-level absence is unambiguous. This directly answers a question the original QA prompt asked but Round 1 didn't check.
- **Midnight/timezone risk (code-level only, not live-verified):** `app/general/page.tsx`'s `parseTimeToday()` builds its target `Date` from the *browser's local* `now.getFullYear()/getMonth()/getDate()` plus the parsed hour/minute — meaning (a) it's inherently local-device-timezone-dependent (fine if every device is physically at the venue, risky if any device has a different OS timezone), and (b) an item scheduled for e.g. "12:30 AM" (intended for after a midnight rollover) would resolve against *today's* date if evaluated before midnight actually ticks over, producing a countdown to a time earlier that same day (i.e., in the past) rather than to the real target. Not reproduced live — this environment has no way to safely fast-forward the system clock across a real midnight boundary — but the code path is unambiguous and matches exactly the scenario the prompt asked about.
- **Memory/longevity:** `performance.memory` on a display tab left open and continuously re-rendering for a large portion of this session's runtime showed no growth (15.5MB → 15.2MB across a sampled interval) — no evidence of an obvious leak, though this is a partial/best-effort check, not a dedicated 20–30 minute isolated monitoring run with DevTools' full memory profiler (not available in this environment).
- **Cross-browser:** only Chromium-based automation was available in this environment — no Firefox, no real mobile Safari. iOS Safari's specific known quirks (dynamic toolbar/100vh, tap-highlight color, input-zoom-on-focus) were not independently verified; flagged as an open question below, same as Round 1's browser-matrix gap.

## Sync/state findings

- **Countdown accuracy under backgrounding/hidden tabs, verified live:** a display tab reported `document.visibilityState === "hidden"` for an extended stretch (an artifact of the browser-automation environment, itself a reasonable stand-in for "phone screen off") and its countdown still advanced by exactly the correct real-elapsed-time delta across two JS-timestamped reads (~11.1s wall-clock → +11s on the displayed countdown) — confirms the timer is computed from real timestamps each render, not accumulated via a naive interval, so it doesn't drift or freeze when backgrounded. This directly answers Persona B's "locks phone for 3 minutes" question in the positive.
- **Public displays recovered from a simulated outage without a manual refresh; the operator dashboard did not.** This is the single most important sync finding of this round (R2-BUG-3) — it inverts the usual assumption that the operator's own screen is the most trustworthy one.
- **Alert dismiss, alert refresh-persistence, and Hold shift-on-resume precision** were all re-confirmed working correctly as byproducts of persona testing (consistent with Round 1) — not re-scored as new findings, just noted as still solid.
- **Optimistic-concurrency retries are invisible to the user in both directions:** a successful-after-retry action and a permanently-failed action look identical on screen (nothing happens visibly either way) — this is the same root issue as R2-BUG-2, called out here because it's specifically a *sync* transparency problem, not just a UI polish one.

## Open questions / things not independently verified this round

- **Real cross-device sync** — still not testable in this environment (see Round 1's identical caveat); this round's "two operators" test used two tabs in the same browser against the same real Supabase project, which exercises the real network/Realtime path but isn't literally two different physical devices.
- **Real mobile Safari** — not available; all mobile testing used a Chromium viewport resize, which does not reproduce iOS-specific quirks (100vh/dynamic toolbar, tap-highlight color, input-zoom-on-focus at small font sizes, safe-area insets). Given this round found real viewport-driven bugs on TV routes at short heights (R2-BUG-4), iOS Safari's dynamic toolbar (which changes the *effective* viewport height as the user scrolls) is a specific, concrete risk worth testing on a real device before an event that uses phones for `/av`/`/green-room` glancing.
- **Real midnight rollover and cross-timezone devices** — reasoned from source code only (see non-bug notes above); this environment has no safe way to fast-forward the system clock or spoof a device timezone against a running dev server.
- **Firefox** — not available in this environment.
- **A true 20–30 minute isolated memory-profiling run** — the partial check performed (see non-bug notes) is suggestive but not conclusive; recommend a real DevTools Performance/Memory recording during a full dress rehearsal if possible.
- **Forced-colors / Windows High Contrast mode** — reasoned from the absence of `forced-colors` CSS, not tested against a real high-contrast environment.

## What a real event night would actually break first — top 3 predictions

Ranked by (likelihood a real crew member does the triggering thing) × (how bad the consequence is once it happens), drawing on everything found across both rounds:

1. **An operator's own dashboard silently lies to them during a network hiccup (R2-BUG-2 + R2-BUG-3 together).** Venue wifi hiccupping for even 10–30 seconds is close to guaranteed at a real multi-hour event. When it happens, Next/Previous/Hold clicks will silently fail with zero feedback, and — worse — the operator's screen can keep silently drifting from reality even *after* the network recovers, requiring a refresh nobody will think to do because nothing on screen suggests anything is wrong. This is the highest-risk finding of either round precisely because it defeats the operator's own trust in their primary tool, and it's a near-certain trigger (any wifi blip) rather than an edge case.
2. **BUG-1 from Round 1 (missing Green Room/General alert), compounded by this round's R2-BUG-1 (Hold silently cancelled by another operator).** Both failure modes share a shape: an operator takes an action they believe is working, and it silently doesn't reach the people who need it — performers waiting for a stage-left call, or an operator whose Hold (probably engaged for a real reason, like a broken mic) gets undone without anyone knowing. Neither requires anything exotic to trigger — just normal show-running under time pressure with more than one person touching the controls, which is exactly how live events actually run.
3. **A performer or crew member checks a TV display mid-transition and gets a genuinely wrong read of the room (R2-BUG-6 finished-session contradiction, plus Round 1's BUG-2 header collapse if anyone's driving from a laptop at a normal-but-unlucky resolution).** "Session Finished" next to a live-looking "05:00 remaining" is a small thing until someone in the green room glances at it between a costume change and confidently tells someone else "we've still got 5 minutes" when the segment is actually over. It's the kind of bug that doesn't crash anything but quietly costs the show a few minutes of confusion at exactly the moments (transitions) where confusion is most expensive.
